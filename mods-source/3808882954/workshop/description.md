[H1]Boeing RC-135V/W "Rivet Joint"[/H1]

[H2]About[/H2]

[i]The RC-135V/W Rivet Joint is a dedicated electronic reconnaissance aircraft used by the U.S. Air Force and Royal Air Force to provide near-real-time signal and electronic intelligence (SIGINT/ELINT). Its onboard sensor suite detects, identifies, and geolocates electromagnetic signals. The onboard crew then distributes collected information through the aircraft's communications suite. Extensively modified from the C-135 airframe, Rivet Joint aircraft are recognizable by their cheek fairings and elongated nose. Due to their role in SIGINT and ELINT, as well as their appearance, the RC-135V/W has aquired the nickname "Snoopy" (SNOOP).
RC-135V/W conversion began around 1973, however the first RC-135 variant (RC-135A) was first produced around 1962. Initially carrying four Pratt & Whitney TF33 engines, the US Air Force began and engine refit for these aircraft in the early 2000's, replacing the TF33's with General Electric CFM56/F108's to increase range, enhance fuel effeciency and reduce maintenance costs.
The current RC-135 aircraft fleet is based out of Offutt Air Force Base, Nebraska, and operated by the 55th Wing; related RC-135 variants include the Cobra Ball (RC-135S) and Combat Sent (RC-135U).[/i]
