SEST R12 - RIBBONS AND NATIONAL AWARDS
10 November 2026 | SEST-DEV

WORLD OPERATIONS - F11 > SERVICE (BETA)
World Operations needs public beta 0.8.7-261107 and SEST SETUP; on the
standard build the world map has no SEST window and these awards do not
appear. 25 fictional world awards recognise manual battle service, distinct
campaign battle days, theatre experience and successful Admiralty duties.
Original awards and v1 service records are preserved. New task records use
separate compatible event keys and never inflate manual battle or USD totals.

Manual battle awards: 3 / 10 / 20 / 50 battles; watch awards: battles on
5 / 15 distinct campaign days; theatre service/veteran: 2 / 5 positioned
battles in each of six theatres; expeditionary/global command: service in
3 / all 6 theatres. These are evidenced service milestones, not assumed
continuous deployments or certificates of unrecorded combat performance.

Admiralty awards: 5 completed accepted tasks; 3 different task types; 3
for each specialist ribbon. A task is a standalone order, or a whole
multi-phase operation counted once when its final phase completes (its phases
do not count separately). Convoy Protection counts convoy-escort orders and
convoy-run operations; Maritime Surveillance counts shadowing orders and
fleet-action operations; Route Security counts convoy-run operations, which
open by clearing the route. Failed, cancelled, declined, lapsed, unaccepted
and other-side outcomes are excluded, and a completion replayed after
reloading a save counts once. SEST records a result while its paper waits
unread in the Admiralty queue: orders read before Runtime 1.6.3 and
operations completed before Runtime 1.6.4 were not recorded and do not count
retroactively. Native task conditions and rewards remain in effect.
Ribbons add no renown, credits or supplies. Auto-resolved battles do not
qualify for manual battle awards; task awards use their own native outcomes.

AUTHORED CAMPAIGNS - TASK FORCE SERVICE RECORD
Commander nations. Southern Watch and Southern Reach keep Australia as the
default and also offer an allied officer in command of the Australian-led
group: US, UK, Japan, New Zealand, France, Canada, South Korea, Spain,
Italy, Germany, the Netherlands, Norway or Denmark. Sulu Line keeps the
Philippines as the default and also offers Thailand or Australia, whose
detachment and frigate serve in its group. Red Line stays Chinese. Choose
the nation in the Service Record when a campaign starts; a commander
already in a save keeps its nation and name.

Each nation has its navy's name, officer ranks and an emblem. Ranks use the
navy's own titles: English for the Commonwealth navies, Korea and Thailand;
French, Spanish, Italian, German, Dutch, Norwegian and Danish titles with
their accents for the others. Commander is OF-4, Captain OF-5 and a
commodore's grade OF-6 in every ladder SEST adds. The Royal Thai Navy has no
commodore's grade, so Captain (Special Grade) is placed at OF-6, level 7, and
Sulu Line's Thai commander holds it. Australia, the US and Japan use the game's own
ranks and emblems. Other emblems come from the SEST gallery flag set: a
naval ensign where the set has one (the UK, New Zealand, Italy), otherwise
the national flag, which is the ensign itself for France, Canada, South
Korea, the Netherlands and Spain. The German, Norwegian, Danish and Thai
ensigns differ from the national flag, and SOURCES.txt says so. New Zealand,
South Korea, the Netherlands, Denmark and Thailand get SEST name pools
(language_en/sest_name_pools.ini); the other nations use the game's own.

The game's same-nation purchase discount follows the commander's nation:
an Australian commander keeps it on the Australian classes; an allied
commander gets it on that nation's classes instead. Each campaign's rules
page gives the class counts. New Zealand has one class, an aircraft sold
with the allied fleet: the RNZAF P-8A. No class is registered to Canada.
Ribbons add no points, renown or supplies.

No campaign offers a Russian commander. SEST Collection Fixes now registers
16 post-1991, Russian-built ship and submarine classes (the Gorshkov and
Grigorovich frigates, the Steregushchiy, Gremyashchiy and Buyan-M corvettes,
the Yasen, Belgorod, Borei and Improved Kilo boats) to the game's Russia
nation, named Russian Federation, instead of the USSR, and SEST
Replenishment's Akademik Pashin registers Russia in its own file, so a
future Russian commander's discount would cover them. Soviet-built hulls,
fictional variants and every Russian aircraft stay Soviet, as stock Cold War
missions use them. The SEST missions fly the Russian flag on all of them, as
before.

Fictional ribbons, any commander: the four completion ribbons (unchanged)
and eight operation ribbons - SW05 Timor Corridor, SW08 Archipelago
Operations, SR12 Antarctic Resupply, TS09 Southern Convoy, RL03 Contact
Intelligence, RL05 Undersea Operations, SL04 Fleet Sustainment and SL06
Littoral Operations. Each needs the mission's required result.

Real-award representations. Personal and service awards go to a commander
of that nation; the unit award goes to the task group whoever commands it.
In Southern Watch and Southern Reach, Australian, US, British, Japanese,
New Zealand, French, Canadian, South Korean, Spanish, German and Danish
commanders receive their own nation's awards; Italian, Dutch and Norwegian
commanders receive the fictional ribbons and the group's Australian
Meritorious Unit Citation only, as their awards' ribbons and criteria could
not be checked against official sources. In Sulu Line, the Philippine awards go to
a Philippine commander only; a Thai or Australian commander receives the
fictional ribbons and the Philippine Republic Presidential Unit Citation.

outstanding Antarctic contribution, not for service there.

RED LINE RUNTIME RIBBONS - BETA RUNTIME R13 ONLY
Two more fictional Red Line ribbons, for any commander, are defined in the
campaign and keyed for SEST's beta runtime R13 (Runtime 1.6.5), which grants
them at the debrief. The game itself ignores the key: R12 (Runtime 1.6.4)
and the standard build never grant them, and neither does R13 with
MissionAwards=0 under [Runtime] in plugins/sest_runtime.ini. Like every
ribbon here, each also needs the mission's required result.
- RL05 Silent Passage: Under the Convergence's Passage objective completed
  with no weapon launched and no gun round fired by any unit of the task
  force. A failed launch counts as a launch; noisemakers, chaff, decoys,
  sonobuoys and CIWS rounds do not. It is granted beside RL05 Undersea
  Operations, which completing the mission earns on its own.
- RLC1 Personnel Recovery: Pilot in the Water's Pilot objective completed
  and at least one survivor recovered, as the debrief's survivors-rescued
  count gives it. The game counts anyone the task force lifts from a raft,
  so the citation says survivors were recovered and names no one.

ART AND SOURCE NOTES
The AOSM-IP ribbon is Nford24's CC0 artwork, copied without modification.
The Distinguished Service Cross, Bronze Star, Commendation Medal and JMSDF
cordon pictures are installed Sea Power pictures, referenced by path; the
JMSDF one is the game's own Defense Memorial Cordon art, as the exact
cordon's colours could not be checked. Every other strip is code-drawn from
the published stripe order. The Canadian decorations use the Regulations'
widths; the NZ Distinguished Service Decoration's and Operational Service
Medal's proportions are measured from official NZDF photographs, as the
Royal Warrants could not be read; the General Campaign Star uses the
widths Canada specifies (DND medal page), its colours sampled from a
Commons image. The Korean ribbons use the Enforcement Decree's widths and
carry the ribbon bar's taegeuk; the Spanish ones use the decrees' widths
and the flag's official colours, the campaign medal's colours sampled from
the official annex drawing. The French ribbons are plain, their colours
sampled from Commons photographs. The German and Danish ribbons follow the
layout their statutes and circulars give, with proportions measured from
Commons drawings, as no official widths were found; the Cross of Honour
carries its gold miniature cross. The Spanish Campaign Medal and the
Bundeswehr Deployment Medal are drawn without the device naming the
operation, which is fictional. The Royal Navy emblem is the White Ensign from the SEST
gallery flag set (public domain). Each campaign's art/ribbons/SOURCES.txt
lists its pictures and sources. No AI imagery is used.

PC CHECKS
On the beta, load an existing world and check Service progress, wrapping
and scrolling. Complete an accepted task, return to the map and compare
task/battle counts.
Start a new Southern Watch campaign: the Service Record offers the fourteen
nations. Compare one Anzac and one Burke price as an Australian and as a US
commander. Check the British commander's empty rank insignia and the White
Ensign emblem. Pick New Zealand, South Korea and Thailand (Sulu Line): each
shows a name from its SEST pool, not a blank, with the given name first as
in the defaults (Seongjin Han), and its emblem. As a New Zealand commander
in Southern Watch, buy the P-8A at Weapons Free at the discounted price; fly
it in a Maritime Patrol slot and note whether it launches, patrols and
lands. Pick Germany or Norway: the rank title's accented letter (the
a-umlaut of Fregattenkapitan, the o-slash of Kommandorkaptein, the level-5
ranks Southern Watch starts at) draws on the commander screen and in a
citation. Open a citation: its date is the mission's, not 1985. As a South
Korean commander, complete SW08: the National Security Medal shows its
taegeuk; as a German one, the Cross of Honour shows its gold cross. Load an
existing save: its commander, nation and earned ribbons are unchanged.
Existing saves are preserved; start a new commander to choose another
nationality.
Russian Federation (SEST Collection Fixes' Nation stubs, not yet seen in
game): in a new Southern Watch campaign the Situation window's Russia block
shows the Project 20380 corvette under the Russian Federation flag and the
Akula under the USSR's. In the Mission Editor, Russian Navy 21's Admiral
Gorshkov shows Russian Federation and the game's own Sovremenny USSR. Pacific
Strike's Action in the Taiwan Strait still generates its Soviet force. The
Southern Watch test card's RU rows have the detail.
On this build, neither Red Line runtime ribbon appears after RL05 or RLC1
and the developer ribbon menu offers neither (the Red Line test card's
checks R5 and R6); that is expected. Once R13 (Runtime 1.6.5) is installed:
complete RL05 without launching anything and RLC1 with its pilot recovered,
and read each runtime ribbon's lines in Player.log (checks R1 to R4). Each
debrief logs two lines per runtime ribbon: judge by the first, the verdict.
The second says "already awarded" after an award, or otherwise repeats the
first. It is expected: Red Line's campaign.ini names itself as its [File]
Base, and the game yields that file once as the open campaign and again as
its base, so R13 reads the key twice.
