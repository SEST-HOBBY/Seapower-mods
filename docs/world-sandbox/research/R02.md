# Global Force Posture and Strategic Installations: A Comprehensive Assessment for Campaign Simulation

## The Architecture of Global Military Logistics

In the contemporary era of multipolar geopolitical competition, the ability to project military power relies entirely on the often-unseen backbone of military logistics. For developers and analysts constructing a high-fidelity campaign simulation, modeling the combat units is only half the equation; the "teeth" cannot operate without the "tail." Strategic support installations, supply depots, and prepositioning networks dictate the operational reach, sustainment tether, and endurance of a nation's military forces.

Modern warfare consumes vast quantities of precision munitions, spare parts, and, most importantly, fuel. As militaries plan for operations across vast maritime distances, particularly in the Indo-Pacific, the concept of "contested logistics" has emerged as a primary concern. Adversaries will actively target supply lines, distribution hubs, and afloat prepositioning ships to starve forward-deployed forces of the resources necessary to fight. The following assessment provides a mapping of major global logistical installations, detailing their supply chains, strategic functions, and the simulation mechanics required to accurately model military sustainment.

## The United States: The Defense Logistics Agency and Expeditionary Sustainment

The United States maintains unparalleled global logistical reach, managed primarily by the Defense Logistics Agency (DLA). The DLA is a massive combat support agency that supplies 86 percent of the military's spare parts and nearly 100 percent of its fuel and troop support consumables. The physical footprint of this supply chain is divided into massive domestic distribution hubs and highly strategic overseas staging areas.

### CONUS Distribution: The Industrial Sustainment Hubs

The continental United States (CONUS) hosts 17 major DLA Distribution centers that serve as the origin points for global military supply chains. The network is anchored by massive primary hubs, notably DLA Distribution Susquehanna in Pennsylvania and DLA Distribution San Joaquin in California, which manage the bulk of cross-country and international materiel routing.

To ensure rapid integration with naval and aerospace combat units, many DLA distribution centers are directly co-located with the military's largest force-generation bases. Major logistical operations are integrated into Naval Station Norfolk in Virginia, Naval Base San Diego in California, Naval Air Station Jacksonville in Florida, and the Puget Sound Naval Shipyard in Washington. Other dedicated supply installations include the Defense Supply Center in Richmond, Virginia (specializing in aircraft parts), and various Army depots such as Anniston, Red River, and Tobyhanna. In a simulation environment, these CONUS hubs represent the ultimate source of replacement airframes, deep-level maintenance components, and bulk munitions.

### OCONUS Distribution and the Afloat Prepositioning Fleet

To circumvent the time delays inherent in trans-oceanic shipping during a sudden crisis, the United States relies on a network of seven overseas (OCONUS) distribution hubs and a fleet of prepositioned ships.

Key overseas land-based hubs include DLA Distribution Yokosuka in Japan, DLA Distribution Guam in the Marianas, and DLA Distribution Pearl Harbor in Hawaii, which collectively form the logistical spine of the Indo-Pacific Command. In the Middle East and Europe, sustainment flows through DLA Distribution Bahrain, DLA Distribution Sigonella in Italy, and DLA Distribution Europe located in Germersheim, Germany.

Complementing these static bases is the Afloat Prepositioning Fleet (APF). These specialized ships are permanently loaded with Marine Corps support cargo, Air Force munitions, hospital equipment, and DLA fuels. They loiter in strategic regions, ready to drastically reduce the response time required to sustain a major theater war. Naval Support Facility Diego Garcia in the British Indian Ocean Territory is a premier hub for this capability; its deepwater lagoon anchors Maritime Prepositioning Squadron ships heavily stocked with equipment, while the base itself serves as a vital logistics hub storing fuel and ammunition out of range of many regional threats.

## The People's Republic of China: The Joint Logistics Support Force (JLSF)

Recognizing historical inadequacies in fragmented supply chains, China underwent a massive military restructuring in 2016 to create the Joint Logistics Support Force (JLSF). Upgraded to a full People's Liberation Army "Arm" in 2024, the JLSF centralizes the sustainment of the Army, Navy, Air Force, and Rocket Force under a unified, theater-level command structure.

### The Wuhan Base and Regional Centers

The JLSF is headquartered at the Wuhan Joint Logistics Support Base in Hubei Province. Wuhan was specifically chosen because it sits at the geographic "weight center" of China and is a major national transportation hub for rail, road, and river networks, allowing for rapid internal lines of communication.

Beneath the Wuhan headquarters, the JLSF operates five Joint Logistics Support Centers (JLSCs), perfectly mirroring the geographic layout of the PLA's five Theater Commands. These centers are located in Wuxi (Eastern Theater), Guilin (Southern Theater), Xining (Western Theater), Shenyang (Northern Theater), and Zhengzhou (Central Theater).

### Specialized Depots and Civil-Military Fusion

The JLSCs manage a vast network of highly specialized facilities designed to sustain high-intensity operations. These include petroleum, oil, and lubricant (POL) depots, oil pipeline groups, field fuel station detachments, ammunition depots, quartermaster depots, and heavy equipment transport units. For a naval simulation focusing on the South China Sea or the Taiwan Strait, the Guilin and Wuxi centers represent the critical logistical anchors that would feed fuel and munitions to the naval bases at Yulin, Longpo, and Ningbo.

Furthermore, the JLSF heavily leverages China's strategy of civil-military fusion. The logistics centers have the authority and the operational mandate to mobilize civilian transport infrastructure—including commercial rail networks, heavy motor transport, air freight, and maritime shipping—to supplement organic military logistics during a conflict.

## Allied Strategic Support and Specialized Nodes

Allied nations also maintain highly specialized logistical and support installations that secure global peripheries and handle sensitive materials.

### Nuclear and Pyrotechnic Logistics: France

France's strategic deterrence relies on highly secure logistical handling at Île Longue, the base for its ballistic missile submarine force. The physical layout of the base is an exercise in extreme logistical security, segregated into color-coded zones. The "Red Zone" encompasses the pyrotechnic facilities and subterranean bunkers where nuclear warheads are stored and maintained. The "Yellow Zone" features the harbor installations, which include a 170-meter-long bunker situated specifically for the secure loading and unloading of nuclear materials into the submarines under the cover of massive roofed docks.

### Remote Outpost Sustainment: United Kingdom

To secure the South Atlantic, the United Kingdom maintains RAF Mount Pleasant in the Falkland Islands. Because of the vast oceanic distances from the British Isles, Mount Pleasant serves as a vital transport and logistics hub. The station's No. 1312 Flight operates an Airbus Voyager KC2 for air-to-air refueling to sustain fighter patrols, alongside an A400M Atlas C1 utilized for tactical transport and supply drops. This aerial logistics node operates in tandem with the nearby Mare Harbour facility, which supports and resupplies the Royal Navy's permanent South Atlantic patrol ships.

## Implications for Campaign Simulation Design

When translating logistical networks into a cohesive, interactive campaign simulation, several strategic dynamics must be modeled to reflect the reality of modern supply chains.

- **Fuel (POL) and Munitions Consumption:** A high-fidelity simulation must enforce rigid consumption rates. Aircraft and ships cannot fight indefinitely; they are tethered to the petroleum, oil, and lubricant (POL) reserves of their respective bases. If the DLA Distribution center in Guam or the JLSF depots under the Wuxi center run dry, the combat effectiveness of the assets stationed there must instantly degrade.
- **Contested Logistics and Interdiction:** Players must be forced to protect their supply lines. The Afloat Prepositioning Fleet offers massive tactical advantages by moving supplies closer to the front, but these slow-moving civilian-crewed vessels are highly vulnerable to submarine attack or long-range strikes. Disrupting these ships, or bombing major rail hubs leading out of Wuhan, should create cascading delays in reinforcement times for frontline bases.
- **Civil-Military Fusion Mechanics:** When playing as the PLA, simulation mechanics should allow the player to activate civilian maritime and rail assets via the JLSCs to rapidly surge logistical capacity, reflecting China's legal and doctrinal framework for utilizing civilian infrastructure during wartime.
