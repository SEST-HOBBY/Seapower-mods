# Global Force Posture and Strategic Installations: A Comprehensive Assessment for Campaign Simulation

## The Architecture of Global Power Projection and Logistics

In the contemporary era of multipolar geopolitical competition, the ability to project military power, secure vital sea lines of communication, and maintain strategic deterrence relies entirely on an interconnected global network of naval and aerospace installations. For developers and analysts constructing a high-fidelity campaign simulation, modeling the combat units is only half the equation; the "teeth" cannot operate without the "tail." Strategic support installations, supply depots, and prepositioning networks dictate the operational reach, sustainment tether, and endurance of a nation's military forces.

Military bases serve as the foundational nodes of operational capability; they are complex ecosystems providing command and control, logistical sustainment, deep-level maintenance, intelligence fusion, and force protection. Modern warfare consumes vast quantities of precision munitions, spare parts, and, most importantly, fuel. As militaries plan for operations across vast maritime distances, particularly in the Indo-Pacific, the concept of "contested logistics" has emerged as a primary concern. The following assessment provides an exhaustive, multi-domain mapping of major global military installations, detailing their resident assets, supply chains, strategic missions, and the simulation mechanics required to accurately model military sustainment and combat.

## The United States: Distributed Lethality and Expeditionary Logistics

The United States maintains the most expansive and technologically advanced network of military installations in human history, designed to facilitate a strategy of forward defense, rapid global response, and sustained expeditionary operations.

### Continental United States: The Industrial and Generation Hubs

The physical footprint, operational throughput, and industrial capacity of United States bases within the continental borders are unparalleled. Occupying approximately 57,000 acres, Naval Base Coronado is the United States Navy's largest installation by physical footprint. It hosts a carrier-capable air station at Naval Air Station North Island, major amphibious training grounds, and environments for Naval Special Warfare. Naval Station Norfolk in Virginia is the largest naval base in the world by operational concentration and fleet density, routinely hosting over 75 ships and 134 aircraft across 11 hangars.

Located in Washington state, Naval Base Kitsap merges the former Naval Station Bremerton and Naval Submarine Base Bangor. It combines massive drydock facilities with the basing of the Pacific Fleet's ballistic-missile submarines. Naval Air Station Patuxent River in Maryland serves as the nerve center for naval aviation research, while Naval Air Station Jacksonville in Florida is a premier hub for anti-submarine warfare, hosting P-8A Poseidons and MQ-4C Tritons.

### OCONUS Distribution and the Afloat Prepositioning Fleet

The projection of United States naval power relies on a vast network of forward-deployed bases managed primarily by the Defense Logistics Agency (DLA), which supplies 86 percent of the military's spare parts and nearly 100 percent of its fuel.

Yokosuka Naval Base in Japan is the headquarters for the United States Seventh Fleet and remains the most strategically vital overseas naval facility in the Indo-Pacific, supported directly by DLA Distribution Yokosuka. Naval Base Guam serves as an unsinkable logistical and aviation hub in the Western Pacific, supported by DLA Distribution Guam. To circumvent time delays inherent in trans-oceanic shipping, the United States relies on the Afloat Prepositioning Fleet (APF). These specialized ships are permanently loaded with Marine Corps support cargo, Air Force munitions, and DLA fuels. They loiter in strategic regions, with Naval Support Facility Diego Garcia acting as a premier deepwater anchorage and bulk fuel hub for these vessels out of range of many regional threats.

## The People's Republic of China: A2/AD, Blue Water Expansion, and Joint Logistics

The People's Liberation Army Navy (PLAN) and the People's Liberation Army Air Force (PLAAF) have systematically constructed a dense network of coastal and island bases designed to enforce rigorous Anti-Access/Area Denial (A2/AD) strategies.

### Southern and Eastern Theater Commands

Hainan Island is the epicenter of Chinese power projection in the southern maritime domain. Yulin Naval Base serves as a heavily fortified base for conventional diesel-electric submarines and smaller surface combatants. Longpo Naval Base (Yulin-East) features massive underground submarine pens carved directly into the coastal mountains to protect China's nuclear-powered ballistic missile submarine fleet. Operating in tandem is Lingshui Airbase, which hosts Shenyang J-15 carrier-capable fighter jets and Shaanxi Y-8Q maritime patrol aircraft. The Eastern Theater Command utilizes Ningbo Naval Base as its headquarters, supporting a massive concentration of surface and subsurface combatants postured almost entirely around a Taiwan contingency.

### The Joint Logistics Support Force (JLSF) and Southeast Asian Expansion

Upgraded to a full PLA "Arm" in 2024, the Joint Logistics Support Force (JLSF) centralizes the sustainment of the Chinese military under a unified command structure. Headquartered at the Wuhan Joint Logistics Support Base in Hubei Province, the JLSF operates five Joint Logistics Support Centers (JLSCs) that manage a vast network of highly specialized facilities, including petroleum depots, ammunition depots, and heavy equipment transport units.

Crucially, China is pushing its logistical and training footprint outward. In a move to expand the PLA's reach into mainland Southeast Asia, China and Laos have opened a joint military aviation training centre at Ban Keun Airport. While designated as a training facility, this marks a new logistical and operational foothold for Chinese air assets well beyond its southwestern border.

## The Russian Federation: Strategic Bastions and Base Survivability

Russian military strategy fundamentally differs from Western expeditionary models, heavily emphasizing layered defense and strategic nuclear deterrence. Located on the Kola Peninsula, Olenya Airbase has emerged as one of the most critical installations for Russian Long-Range Aviation. It serves as a sanctuary and launch point for Tu-22M3, Tu-95MS, and Tu-160 bombers. Engels-2 Airbase is Russia's premier strategic bomber base and the heart of its European aviation deterrence. To mitigate pervasive drone threats, Russia has increasingly relocated assets to bases deep within Siberia and the Far East, such as Ukrainka Airbase and Belaya Airbase.

## Independent Geostrategic Clusters and Allied Nodes

Beyond the immediate borders of superpowers, several highly concentrated, independent geographic clusters act as global chokepoints and essential force multipliers.

### The Horn of Africa: The Djibouti Cluster

Situated at the entrance to the Red Sea and the Bab-el-Mandeb strait, Djibouti hosts the densest cluster of foreign military bases in the world. This small nation leverages its geography to host rival global powers within miles of one another. The United States operates Camp Lemonnier, a former French Foreign Legion base that now hosts 4,500 personnel and serves as the primary operational headquarters for U.S. Africa Command in the region.

Just down the coast, China operates the PLA Support Base, its very first overseas military installation, established at a cost of $590 million. France maintains a significant presence at Base Aérienne 188 (also known as the Héron naval base), one of its largest military installations abroad. Both Japan and Italy also operate their own dedicated defense force bases in Djibouti, utilizing the location for anti-piracy, counterterrorism, and intelligence gathering across the Red Sea and the broader Indo-Pacific.

### The Australian Projection

To anchor the southern hemisphere and the Indo-Pacific, Australia maintains highly strategic isolated installations. HMAS Stirling, located on Garden Island near Perth in Western Australia, serves as Fleet Base West. It is the cornerstone of the Royal Australian Navy's Indian Ocean presence and submarine operations. For aerospace power projection, RAAF Base Tindal, located in the remote Northern Territory, acts as a primary military air base capable of hosting strike fighters and long-range allied bomber deployments.

### European and South Atlantic Support

Located in Cyprus, RAF Akrotiri serves as a crucial forward operating and mounting base for the United Kingdom in the Eastern Mediterranean, frequently used for search and rescue operations and regional crisis staging. To secure the South Atlantic, the UK maintains RAF Mount Pleasant in the Falkland Islands, utilizing an Airbus Voyager KC2 for vital air-to-air refueling to sustain fighter patrols, alongside an A400M Atlas C1 for tactical supply drops. This aerial logistics node operates in tandem with the nearby Mare Harbour facility, which supports the Royal Navy's permanent South Atlantic patrol ships.

In the High North, Norway operates Evenes Air Station to monitor the GIUK Gap, hosting P-8A Poseidons and F-35A interceptors. Japan's JMSDF operates from nodes like Naha and Atsugi, utilizing the highly advanced Kawasaki P-1 to create an immense anti-submarine web over the First Island Chain. France ensures its strategic independence via Île Longue, the base for its ballistic missile submarine force, utilizing an incredibly secure, color-coded logistical network to manage and load nuclear warheads outside of satellite view.

## Implications for Campaign Simulation Design

When translating logistical networks and combat platforms into a cohesive, interactive campaign simulation, several strategic dynamics must be modeled to reflect the reality of modern supply chains and warfare.

- **Fuel (POL) and Munitions Consumption:** A high-fidelity simulation must enforce rigid consumption rates. Aircraft and ships cannot fight indefinitely; they are tethered to the petroleum, oil, and lubricant (POL) reserves of their respective bases. If the DLA Distribution center in Guam or the JLSF depots under the Wuxi center run dry, combat effectiveness must instantly degrade.
- **Contested Logistics and Interdiction:** Players must be forced to protect their supply lines. The Afloat Prepositioning Fleet offers massive tactical advantages by moving supplies closer to the front, but these vessels are highly vulnerable. Disrupting these ships, or bombing major rail hubs leading out of Wuhan, should create cascading delays in reinforcement times for frontline bases.
- **The End of Sanctuary and Base Defense:** The proliferation of long-range, autonomous strike systems has permanently nullified the concept of a safe rear area. Players cannot simply park high-value assets at a home base and assume they are safe. A successful simulation engine must require players to dynamically manage base defense, construct hardened aircraft shelters, and invest heavily in point-defense systems.
- **Neutrality and Multi-Faction Clusters:** In dense geostrategic hubs like Djibouti, simulations must handle rules of engagement carefully. Players operating out of Camp Lemonnier must navigate the immediate presence of the Chinese PLA Support Base without triggering unintended escalation, modeling the complex espionage and diplomatic tensions inherent in these shared strategic chokepoints.
