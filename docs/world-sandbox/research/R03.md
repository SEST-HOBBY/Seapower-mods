# Global Force Posture and Strategic Installations: A Comprehensive Assessment for Campaign Simulation

## The Architecture of Global Power Projection

In the contemporary era of multipolar geopolitical competition, the ability to project military power, secure vital sea lines of communication, and maintain strategic deterrence relies entirely on an interconnected global network of naval and aerospace installations. For developers and analysts constructing a high-fidelity campaign simulation modeling modern statecraft, conventional warfare, or strategic deterrence, understanding the physical infrastructure that underpins global force posture is essential. Military bases serve as the foundational nodes of operational capability; they are complex ecosystems providing command and control, logistical sustainment, deep-level maintenance, intelligence fusion, and force protection.

The geographic distribution of these installations dictates the operational reach, response times, and sustainment tether of a nation's military forces. Strategic chokepoints, such as the Strait of Malacca, the Greenland-Iceland-United Kingdom Gap, and the Strait of Hormuz, are monitored and contested from nearby forward-operating locations. Furthermore, the advent of long-range precision fires, hypersonic glide vehicles, and autonomous asymmetric drone warfare has fundamentally altered the survivability of these installations. Consequently, militaries are being forced to adapt their force postures, disperse their high-value assets, and harden their infrastructure. The following assessment provides an exhaustive, multi-domain mapping of major global military installations, detailing their resident assets, strategic missions, and the cascading geopolitical and tactical implications required to build a comprehensive global campaign simulation.

## The United States: Distributed Lethality and Expeditionary Logistics

The United States maintains the most expansive and technologically advanced network of military installations in human history, designed to facilitate a strategy of forward defense, rapid global response, and sustained expeditionary operations. This network is bifurcated into massive continental hubs that serve as force generation and sustainment centers, and a complex web of forward-deployed overseas facilities that enable immediate power projection into contested theaters.

### Continental United States: The Industrial and Generation Hubs

The physical footprint, operational throughput, and industrial capacity of United States bases within the continental borders are unparalleled. These bases represent the logistical backbone of the United States Navy, Marine Corps, and allied coalition forces.

Occupying approximately 57,000 acres, Naval Base Coronado is the United States Navy's largest installation by physical footprint. Located in California, it is a massive aerospace-industrial complex that supports a population of over 36,000 military and civilian personnel. Coronado is strategically unique in that it hosts a carrier-capable air station at Naval Air Station North Island, major amphibious training grounds at the Naval Amphibious Base Coronado, and specialized, high-end environments for Naval Special Warfare. Naval Air Station North Island serves as a primary embarkation point for Pacific-bound Carrier Strike Groups and hosts large concentrations of MH-60S and MH-60R Seahawk helicopters, alongside F/A-18 Super Hornets and F-35C Lightning IIs belonging to Carrier Air Wings such as CVW-2, CVW-9, CVW-11, and CVW-17. Simulating operations from Coronado requires accounting for complex air traffic control, carrier qualification cycles, and the integration of special operations deployments.

While significantly smaller in physical land area, spanning between 4,000 and 6,000 acres, Naval Station Norfolk in Virginia is the largest naval base in the world by operational concentration and fleet density. Serving as the headquarters for the United States Navy's Fleet Forces Command, Norfolk boasts 11 miles of pier and wharf space, routinely hosting over 75 ships and 134 aircraft across 11 hangars. Norfolk operates as the primary force generation hub for the Atlantic, Mediterranean, and Middle Eastern theaters. The logistical orchestration required to manage the maintenance, supply, and deployment cycles of its resident nuclear-powered aircraft carriers and escorting destroyer squadrons provides a dense framework for simulation mechanics surrounding fleet readiness and turnaround times.

Located in the western Puget Sound of Washington state, Naval Base Kitsap merges the former Naval Station Bremerton and Naval Submarine Base Bangor, covering approximately 12,000 acres. It is strategically vital because it combines the massive drydock and maintenance facilities capable of servicing Nimitz-class aircraft carriers at the Puget Sound Naval Shipyard with the basing of the Pacific Fleet's ballistic-missile submarines. Kitsap houses one of two major United States Navy nuclear weapons facilities, making it a critical node in America's nuclear triad and strategic deterrence posture in the Indo-Pacific.

The nerve center for naval aviation research, development, and testing is Naval Air Station Patuxent River in Maryland. Encompassing 6,400 acres and employing over 17,000 personnel, it hosts the Naval Air Systems Command and the Naval Test Pilot School. Every major naval aircraft, from the P-8A Poseidon to F-35 variants and emerging unmanned systems, undergoes rigorous flight and weapons integration testing here, making it a critical intelligence and technological node. Further south, Naval Air Station Jacksonville in Florida occupies 3,800 acres and serves as a premier hub for anti-submarine warfare. It hosts over 100 aircraft, including P-8A Poseidons, MH-60R helicopters, C-130T Hercules tactical transports, and MQ-4C Triton maritime patrol drones, focusing heavily on tracking adversary submarines in the Atlantic and Caribbean. To simulate pilot pipelines, Naval Air Station Whiting Field in Florida must be included, as it conducts over sixty percent of all primary flight training for the Navy, Marine Corps, and Coast Guard, housing roughly 250 primary training aircraft.

### Overseas Contingency and Forward Deployed Naval Forces

The projection of United States naval power relies on a vast network of forward-deployed bases that drastically reduce transit times to global hotspots, enabling immediate crisis response.

Yokosuka Naval Base in Japan is the headquarters for the United States Seventh Fleet and remains the most strategically vital overseas naval facility for the United States in the Indo-Pacific. It is the only overseas base globally that permanently hosts a forward-deployed aircraft carrier strike group. This group is currently centered around the USS George Washington, which assumed the role from the USS Ronald Reagan. Operating in tandem with Yokosuka is Naval Base Guam, situated in Apra Harbor, alongside the adjacent Andersen Air Force Base. Guam serves as an unsinkable logistical and aviation hub in the Western Pacific, allowing for the rapid dispatch of naval assets, strategic bombers, and Marine expeditionary forces to the South China Sea, the Taiwan Strait, and the Korean Peninsula. Because it is sovereign United States territory, Guam provides immense strategic flexibility unconstrained by host-nation basing agreements.

In the British Indian Ocean Territory, the highly secretive and strategic base of Naval Support Facility Diego Garcia operates as a vital logistics and bomber hub. Located safely out of range of many regional theater ballistic missile threats—situated 3,800 kilometers from Iran and 4,700 kilometers from Beijing—its deepwater lagoon anchors Maritime Prepositioning Squadron ships heavily stocked with equipment for ground invasions. Its airfield routinely accommodates strategic bombers including the B-52, B-1B, and B-2 stealth platforms, making it a critical node for modeling long-range strike missions into the Middle East and Asia.

In the Middle East, Naval Support Activity Bahrain serves as the headquarters for the United States Naval Forces Central Command and the Fifth Fleet, orchestrating operations in the Persian Gulf, the Red Sea, and the Arabian Sea. This facility is supported by aviation assets operating out of Muharraq Airfield and logistics nodes in Kuwait, Oman, and the United Arab Emirates, specifically the Jebel Ali Port Facility.

The United States European Command maintains a highly dispersed footprint. Naval Station Rota in Spain serves as a critical gateway between the Atlantic and the Mediterranean, hosting forward-deployed Arleigh Burke-class destroyers integrated into the NATO missile defense shield. Further east, Naval Support Activity Naples and Naval Air Station Sigonella in Italy provide command, control, and maritime patrol coverage across the Mediterranean and North Africa. Sigonella, alongside Naval Support Activity Souda Bay in Greece, forms the backbone of Mediterranean anti-submarine and intelligence operations. Additionally, the simulation must account for the Aegis Ashore missile defense sites located at Naval Support Facility Deveselu in Romania and Naval Support Facility Redzikowo in Poland, which provide critical ballistic missile defense coverage for the European continent. Finally, United States Africa Command relies heavily on Camp Lemonnier in Djibouti, a critical node for counter-terrorism, drone operations, and maritime security at the Bab-el-Mandeb strait.

### Carrier Strike Group Dynamics and Simulation Mechanics

The deployment cycles of United States aircraft carriers are inextricably linked to these base infrastructures, providing a dynamic element for any campaign simulation. Carriers operate on strict cycles of maintenance, training, and deployment. For example, the USS Nimitz operates out of Kitsap-Bremerton but undergoes homeport shifts and deployments through San Diego. The USS Dwight D. Eisenhower and the USS Harry S. Truman stage from Norfolk for Atlantic and Middle Eastern deployments, conducting intensive Carrier Qualifications off the Virginia Capes and Jacksonville operating areas prior to deployment.

The Pacific power projection is spearheaded by the USS Carl Vinson and the USS Abraham Lincoln, both homeported in San Diego, which conduct Tailored Ship's Training Availability and Final Evaluation Problems off the Southern California coast before transiting west. The USS Theodore Roosevelt recently departed San Diego to relieve other carriers in the Central Command area of responsibility. Simultaneously, carriers like the USS John C. Stennis undergo years-long Refueling and Complex Overhauls at facilities like Newport News Shipbuilding, removing them from the strategic chessboard for extended periods. A high-fidelity simulation must model these maintenance periods, transit times, and training work-ups, forcing players to manage a limited pool of ready carrier assets against emerging global crises.

## The Russian Federation: Strategic Bastions, Long-Range Aviation, and Base Survivability

Russian military strategy fundamentally differs from Western expeditionary models, heavily emphasizing layered defense, strategic nuclear deterrence, and the exploitation of vast geographic depth. The Russian maritime and aerospace posture is meticulously designed to protect its ballistic missile submarines in heavily defended coastal "bastions," such as the Barents Sea and the Sea of Okhotsk, while utilizing Long-Range Aviation to threaten adversary infrastructure from a standoff distance.

### The High North and the Kola Peninsula

Located on the Kola Peninsula, 92 kilometers south of Murmansk, Olenya Airbase has emerged as one of the most critical installations for Russian Long-Range Aviation and a primary node for strikes against Ukraine. Initially established as an Arctic staging facility for nuclear strikes during the Cold War—famously launching the Tu-95V that dropped the Tsar Bomba in 1961—Olenya is highly strategic due to its proximity to the GIUK Gap, the North Atlantic, and the Norwegian border.

The base serves as the home of the 40th Composite Aviation Regiment, operating Tu-22M3 bombers in maritime-attack and land-strike roles. Recently, due to profound vulnerabilities deeper inside European Russia, Olenya has seen a massive influx of strategic bombers seeking sanctuary. Satellite imagery from 2024 and 2025 confirmed the presence of dozens of aircraft on the tarmac, including up to 35 Tu-22M3 supersonic bombers, 10 Tu-95MS turboprop strategic bombers, and several Tu-160 missile carriers, alongside An-12 transport aircraft. Bombers from Olenya routinely fly extended combat sorties southward to launch standoff cruise missiles, capitalizing on the base's distance from the Ukrainian border—over 2,000 kilometers—to maximize reaction times for air defenses while attempting to keep the precious airframes safe.

However, the assumption of Arctic sanctuary is rapidly eroding. In a series of operations, most notably Operation Spider's Web in June 2025, Ukrainian long-range autonomous drone swarms successfully navigated the immense distance to strike Olenya, damaging Tu-22M3 and Tu-95MS airframes. This introduces a critical mechanic for campaign simulations: geographic distance no longer guarantees immunity from asymmetric strike capabilities.

### Strategic Depth, Attrition, and Vulnerability

Engels-2 Airbase, located 14 kilometers east of Saratov, is Russia's premier strategic bomber base and the heart of its European aviation deterrence. It hosts the 22nd Guards Heavy Bomber Aviation Division, comprising the 121st Guards Heavy Bomber Aviation Regiment, which operates the Tu-160M, and the 184th Heavy Bomber Aviation Regiment, operating the Tu-95MS. The facility features a massive 3,500-metre concrete runway and extensive munitions storage designed to support sustained strategic bombardment. As the primary launch point for standoff missile strikes into Ukraine, Engels-2 has been repeatedly targeted. Drones have caused catastrophic damage, completely destroying Tu-95MS bombers and prompting the Russian Aerospace Forces to construct hardened aircraft shelters and disperse their fleets to secondary airfields.

To mitigate this pervasive drone threat, Russia has increasingly relocated assets to bases deep within Siberia and the Far East. Ukrainka Airbase in the Amur region is a major hub for Tu-95MS operations facing the Pacific. Belaya Airbase, located roughly 4,500 kilometers from the Ukrainian border in the Irkutsk Oblast, has historically hosted up to 42 Tu-22M3 bombers. However, even these remote installations have fallen victim to creative long-range drone strikes, demonstrating that Russia's sheer geographic size is an insufficient defense against autonomous loitering munitions.

The intensive operational tempo of the ongoing war has taken a severe toll on these aging strategic airframes. This attrition was starkly highlighted when a Tu-95MS suffered a catastrophic multi-engine failure and crashed upon takeoff from Ukrainka, resulting in six fatalities. Simulating the Russian aviation posture requires modeling this severe logistical and technical degradation; forcing players to balance the tactical need for standoff strikes against the reality of airframe fatigue, engine failure probabilities, and the logistical strain of operating from remote Far East airfields.

## The People's Republic of China: A2/AD and Blue Water Expansion

The People's Liberation Army Navy (PLAN) and the People's Liberation Army Air Force (PLAAF) have systematically constructed a dense network of coastal and island bases designed to enforce rigorous Anti-Access/Area Denial (A2/AD) strategies within the First and Second Island Chains. Simultaneously, these bases are laying the logistical groundwork for a truly global, blue-water navy capable of out-of-area operations.

### The Southern Theater Command: The South China Sea Bastion

The Southern Theater Command holds operational responsibility for the South China Sea, viewing it as a critical maritime bastion essential for the survival of China's growing nuclear submarine fleet and an optimal operational space for its expanding surface fleet. The headquarters of the South Sea Fleet is located in Guangdong, but the operational spear tip is Hainan Island.

Hainan Island is the epicenter of Chinese power projection in the southern maritime domain. Yulin Naval Base, located in the eastern suburbs of Sanya, serves as a traditional, heavily fortified base for conventional diesel-electric submarines and smaller surface combatants, including elements of the PLAN Marine Corps Special Operations Brigade. Further east on Yalong Bay sits the highly strategic Longpo Naval Base, formerly known as Yulin-East. This deep-water facility is the primary harbor for the PLAN's nuclear-powered ballistic missile submarines (SSBNs) and is increasingly outfitted to support China's expanding fleet of aircraft carriers. The infrastructure here is formidable, featuring massive underground submarine pens carved directly into the coastal mountains, designed to protect the SSBN fleet from preemptive conventional or nuclear strikes.

Operating in direct tandem with these naval facilities is Lingshui Airbase, situated in the Lingshui Li Autonomous County on Hainan. As a highly strategic naval air station, it sits within easy striking distance of disputed territories and artificial island outposts across the South China Sea. Lingshui has undergone extensive upgrades and currently hosts Shenyang J-15 carrier-capable fighter jets, as well as critical reconnaissance and airborne early warning aircraft, specifically the Shaanxi KJ-500 and the anti-submarine warfare-optimized Shaanxi Y-8Q. The mix of aircraft indicates Lingshui's dual role: providing immediate land-based air cover for the PLAN fleet and conducting continuous intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance sweeps to track foreign naval assets, such as United States carrier strike groups, entering the South China Sea.

### The Eastern Theater Command: Taiwan Strait Readiness

The Eastern Theater Command is postured almost entirely around a Taiwan contingency and controlling the East China Sea. Ningbo Naval Base, located on the coast of Zhejiang province, serves as the headquarters for the Eastern Theater Command Navy, formerly known as the East Sea Fleet. Established in 1955, Ningbo supports a massive concentration of surface and subsurface combatants. Resident units include the 3rd and 6th Destroyer Zhidui (squadrons), the 8th Frigate Dadui, the 21st Fastboat Zhidui, and the 22nd and 42nd Submarine Zhidui.

This naval concentration is intimately integrated with naval aviation assets under the command's umbrella, including the 4th and 6th Air Divisions and the 1st Flying Panther Regiment, alongside dedicated shore-based radar brigades. In a simulation environment, Ningbo represents the primary force generation node for any amphibious assault or maritime blockade of Taiwan. Players commanding this theater would utilize these clustered assets to form a dense, multi-domain strike complex designed to achieve rapid local sea control and physically deny intervention by external naval forces through a saturation of anti-ship cruise missiles and coordinated submarine ambushes.

## Allied Anti-Submarine Warfare and Strategic Chokepoints

A comprehensive global campaign cannot be modeled solely around superpower dynamics. Regional powers and allied networks possess highly specialized, technologically advanced installations that act as crucial force multipliers. These bases monitor geographic chokepoints, enforce regional deterrence, and establish impenetrable anti-submarine nets.

### The GIUK Gap and the High North: Norway's Sentinel

Located in Nordland county above the Arctic Circle, Evenes Air Station is a cornerstone of NATO's maritime domain awareness in the High North. Operated by the Royal Norwegian Air Force's 133 Air Wing, the base recently underwent a massive 8 billion NOK upgrade to support next-generation aircraft. Evenes hosts the 333 Squadron, which operates a fleet of five Boeing P-8A Poseidon maritime patrol aircraft, ceremonially named Vingtor, Viking, Ulabrand, Hugin, and Munin, delivered between 2021 and 2022. These highly advanced aircraft are specifically tasked with hunting Russian submarines attempting to transit from their Kola Peninsula bastions through the Greenland-Iceland-United Kingdom (GIUK) Gap into the open Atlantic.

Evenes also functions as a critical Quick Reaction Alert (QRA) base for Norwegian F-35A Lightning IIs, taking over the role previously held by F-16s at Bodø. These F-35s routinely scramble—often dozens of times a year—to intercept and escort Russian Tu-142 maritime patrol aircraft and Tu-95 bombers operating in international airspace over the Barents Sea. The strategic value of Evenes is drastically amplified by its recent designation as an "agreed area" under a defense agreement with the United States. This allows United States and United Kingdom P-8A Poseidons, as well as RC-135 Rivet Joint intelligence aircraft, to stage seamlessly from the facility. This interoperability creates a unified, multinational ASW kill web. Demonstrating the extreme operational reach provided by the base, a British P-8A recently staged from Evenes to conduct the first-ever Poseidon flyover of the North Pole. Due to its immense strategic value, the station is heavily protected by a dedicated air defense battalion equipped with the NASAMS III weapon system and specialized counter-UAS technologies.

### The First Island Chain: Japan's Maritime Patrol Network

The Japan Maritime Self-Defense Force (JMSDF) operates a highly distributed and specialized naval aviation network focused almost entirely on anti-submarine warfare and maritime surveillance, aimed squarely at countering both Russian Pacific Fleet movements and the expanding Chinese submarine force.

The JMSDF's Fleet Air Force operates from several key nodes. Fleet Air Wing 1 operates out of Kanoya Air Base, while Fleet Air Wing 4 operates from Naval Air Facility Atsugi. Fleet Air Wing 5 is positioned at Naha in Okinawa, providing extreme proximity to the First Island Chain and the Taiwan Strait. These bases are currently in the process of transitioning from the legacy Kawasaki-built P-3C Orion to the Kawasaki P-1, an advanced, domestically produced, four-engine maritime patrol jet.

The Kawasaki P-1 represents a massive leap in indigenous ASW capability. Powered by four podded IHI Corporation F7-10 turbofan engines producing 13,000 lbf of thrust each, it boasts a top speed of 996 km/h and an operational range of 8,000 kilometers. The P-1 holds the distinction of being the first operational aircraft globally to utilize a fly-by-optics control system. By replacing traditional metal wiring with optical fibers, the aircraft drastically reduces internal electromagnetic interference, allowing its highly sensitive sensors—which include a Toshiba HPS-106 Active Electronically Scanned Array (AESA) radar and a CAE-built magnetic anomaly detector (MAD) embedded in the tail—to operate with unprecedented fidelity.

With 34 to 36 units currently in service and more on order, P-1s flying from Atsugi and Kanoya drop dense networks of sonobuoys (capable of carrying 30+ pre-loaded and 70+ internally deployable buoys) to track submarine acoustic signatures. The aircraft features an artificial intelligence (AI) system designed to assist the Tactical Coordinator (TACCO) by automatically calculating the optimal flight course to prosecute a submarine contact. Its internal bomb bay can carry up to 9,000 kg of weaponry, including Mk.46 and Type 97 torpedoes, while wing hardpoints support AGM-84 Harpoon and ASM-1C anti-ship missiles. The aggressive posture of these aircraft was highlighted in December 2018 when a South Korean Navy destroyer allegedly locked its fire-control radar onto a P-1 from Atsugi's 3rd Squadron, triggering a major diplomatic incident. Simulating the JMSDF requires modeling this high-end sensor integration and the rapid response times enabled by bases like Naha.

## Regional Deterrence and Isolated Outposts

Beyond the major theaters of the Indo-Pacific and Eastern Europe, remote and specialized bases provide critical capabilities that secure global peripheries and maintain continuous strategic deterrence.

### The South Atlantic Sentinel: The Falkland Islands

To secure the South Atlantic and project power into the Antarctic region, the United Kingdom maintains RAF Mount Pleasant in the Falkland Islands. Opened in 1985 by Prince Andrew following the Falklands War, it is a joint-force complex heavily fortified against regional threats, boasting a 2,589-meter primary asphalt runway. The base acts as the Falkland Islands' only international airport while serving its primary military function.

The station provides a base for air-defense and transport operations, commanded by the No. 905 Expeditionary Air Wing. The wing comprises the No. 1435 Flight, which operates four Eurofighter Typhoon FGR4s (specifically the Tranche 1 variant) to provide constant air defense for the Falklands, South Sandwich Islands, and South Georgia. Because of the vast oceanic distances in the South Atlantic, these interceptors are sustained by the No. 1312 Flight, operating an Airbus Voyager KC2 for vital air-to-air refueling, alongside an A400M Atlas C1 utilized for tactical transport, search and rescue, and maritime patrol. Ground-based air defense for the complex is provided by a detachment of the 7th Air Defence Group operating the highly advanced Sky Sabre missile system.

Operating in seamless conjunction with the airbase is the nearby Mare Harbour facility, which hosts the Royal Navy's permanent South Atlantic patrol ship. Since 2020, this role has primarily been filled by HMS Forth (P222), a Batch 2 River-class offshore patrol vessel. Entering service in 2018, HMS Forth conducts counter-piracy, fishery protection, and sovereignty patrols across the South Atlantic. Utilizing its 5,500 nautical mile range, a top speed of 25 knots, and an endurance of 35 days, HMS Forth polices a massive maritime territory, often operating alongside the Typhoons from Mount Pleasant to intercept unregistered vessels or escort foreign military assets.

### The European Nuclear Redoubt: France

The strategic independence of France relies entirely on its continuous at-sea deterrence posture, anchored by a single, highly specialized base. Located on a peninsula in the roadstead of Brest, Île Longue is the most secretive and heavily defended military installation in the French Republic. It serves as the exclusive base for the Marine Nationale's Force Océanique Stratégique—the ballistic missile submarine force.

Hosting four Triomphant-class nuclear submarines, the facility's physical layout is an exercise in extreme security, strictly segregated into color-coded zones. The Blue Zone manages general security, administration, and houses the Fusiliers Marins responsible for the perimeter defense of the site. The Red Zone contains heavily guarded pyrotechnic facilities and subterranean bunkers where nuclear warheads are stored and maintained. The Yellow Zone features the harbor installations, characterized by two massive 200-meter-long roofed docks. These covered docks are designed to completely obscure the submarines from overhead satellite surveillance during maintenance and loading, ensuring adversaries cannot easily track the deployment cycles of the SSBNs. A 170-meter-long bunker situated between the docks facilitates the secure loading and unloading of nuclear materials. The base has recently undergone extensive modifications to support the next-generation M51 submarine-launched ballistic missile.

## Second and Third-Order Implications for Campaign Simulation Design

When translating this massive array of geographical, technical, and logistical data into a cohesive, interactive campaign simulation, the relationships between bases, platforms, and geography generate critical second and third-order strategic dynamics that must be mathematically modeled.

### The End of Sanctuary and Infrastructure Hardening

Historically, bases located deep within a nation's interior or far from the immediate front lines were treated as inviolable sanctuaries. The proliferation of long-range, autonomous strike systems has permanently nullified this concept. The successful Ukrainian drone strikes on Russia's Engels-2 and Belaya airbases—located hundreds or thousands of kilometers from the border—demonstrate that static infrastructure is highly vulnerable to relatively cheap, asymmetric technologies.

In a simulation environment, this implies that players cannot simply park high-value assets like Tu-160s or B-2 bombers at a home base and assume they are safe. A successful simulation engine must require players to dynamically manage base defense. Players must be forced to allocate resources to disperse their forces, construct hardened aircraft shelters, and invest heavily in point-defense systems (like the NASAMS III stationed at Evenes or the Sky Sabre at Mount Pleasant) to protect their strategic hubs. Furthermore, the continuous redeployment of Russian bombers to the Arctic base of Olenya illustrates the severe logistical strain of base vulnerability. Moving assets out of immediate drone range protects the airframes but drastically increases flight times to target areas. In a simulation, this must translate to increased fuel consumption, accelerated wear on airframes, higher maintenance failure probabilities (as evidenced by the Tu-95MS crash at Ukrainka), and providing adversary early-warning systems significantly more time to detect incoming strikes.

### The Sensor-Shooter ASW Network

The simulation of maritime environments must heavily feature the interlocking, multinational anti-submarine warfare networks established by allied nations. Submarines are no longer hunted by single platforms operating in a vacuum; they are targeted by a distributed kill web. A Russian submarine departing the Kola Peninsula must first evade Norwegian P-8A Poseidons staging from Evenes. Because Evenes integrates seamlessly with United Kingdom and United States aircraft, target data is handed off across allied platforms via data links, requiring the simulation to model shared situational awareness rather than individual unit vision cones.

Similarly, in the Pacific, the Chinese submarine fleet operating out of Yulin and Longpo faces a dense curtain of acoustic and electromagnetic sensors. The JMSDF's Kawasaki P-1s, utilizing their fly-by-optics and AESA radars, operate from Kanoya and Atsugi to drop complex sonobuoy barriers across the First Island Chain. To simulate this accurately, the game engine must account for the loiter times of these aircraft, acoustic propagation through different ocean thermal layers, and the electromagnetic emission control (EMCON) strategies of the submarines attempting to pierce these nets. The presence of Chinese J-15s and Y-8Qs from Lingshui Airbase acting as a counter-screen to allied ASW efforts creates a complex, multi-layered aerial battle that must be resolved before a single torpedo is fired.

### Logistical Tethering and Attrition

Ultimately, the ability to sustain combat operations is strictly tethered to the logistical capacity of a base. Diego Garcia and Guam are prime examples; while they provide massive operational reach for United States bombers and surface fleets, they require constant resupply via vulnerable sea lines of communication. In a conflict scenario, an adversary would not necessarily need to destroy these bases outright. Heavily mining the approaches to Apra Harbor or repeatedly harassing logistical shipping with conventional diesel-electric submarines from Yulin could effectively neutralize the combat effectiveness of the forward-deployed forces by starving them of fuel and munitions. The operational readiness of complex platforms like the P-1, P-8A, or F-35 is entirely dependent on an uninterrupted flow of spare parts. Campaign simulations must move beyond simple unit spawning and enforce rigid supply chain mechanics, where the isolation of a base directly degrades the combat power of its resident units.
