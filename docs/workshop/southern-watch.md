[h1]Southern Watch - A Definitive Modernised Seapower Campaign[/h1]

September 2026. A red lodgement in Papua and a sanctions war across the
archipelago have turned Australia's northern approaches into a front line.
Thirteen short missions, each one problem with a way to win and a way to
lose, sized for about forty minutes.

[h2]Missions[/h2]
[olist]
[*][b]Sanctioned Cargo[/b] - BANDA SEA, first light. Three hulls broke out of the Bayu-Undan anchorage overnight under a sanctioned flag and are running east for the Seram passage. Two are lifts.
[*][b]Warramunga's Shot[/b] - TIMOR SEA. A red surface action group is working south-west out of the Sunrise field, screening the second sanctioned convoy.
[*][b]Narco Transit[/b] - TIMOR CORRIDOR, middle watch. Border Force has a pattern: something small and very quiet runs this line twice a week, snorting at periscope depth between the fishing fleets, and nothing with a hull number has ever caught it.
[*][b]Mogami's Corner[/b] - BANDA SEA, last hour before midnight. A Type 093B went quiet somewhere in this box six hours ago and has not been heard since.
[*][b]Triton's Picture[/b] - ARAFURA SEA. HOBART has the missiles and no idea where to put them. The Triton has the picture and nothing to shoot with. Between them that is a working kill chain, and it holds exactly as long as the drone does.
[*][b]Viper Zero[/b] - OFF KALIMANTAN. A Type 071 is standing in toward the Chinese industrial park with a 054A and a 056A screening her, and whatever is in her well deck is meant to be ashore by nightfall.
[*][b]Rafale, Timor Gap[/b] - TIMOR GAP, late afternoon, weather closing. Two Russian frigates have been shadowing the southern lane for a week and have now turned toward the Darwin approaches. The 22350 is a small ship with a serious air-defence suite and no reason to be here.
[*][b]Foxhound Sweep[/b] - HIGH OVER THE ARAFURA. The Wedgetail and the tanker are the reason the allied air picture exists out here, and both of them are slow, large and unable to defend themselves.
[*][b]Black Widow Debut[/b] - CERAM SEA, high. A KJ-500 has been orbiting north of the Banda for two days, and every red intercept in this half of the archipelago has been run off its picture.
[*][b]Fujian's Shadow[/b] - NORTH OF THE BANDA SEA. FORD is 200 nautical miles south of the Fujian group and both sides know it. The red carrier has been cycling deck launches for an hour and the pattern has stopped looking like training: two sweeps forward, then a stream behind them.
[*][b]The Biak Regiment[/b] - BIAK, 0430. A Russian S-400 regiment sits on the airfield with its big radar up, and while it is up nothing allied flies the northern route. The airfield has a modern fighter regiment on it as well.\n\nYou have Growlers and Eagles out of the forward field.
[*][b]Tigers over Papua[/b] - PAPUA, before dawn. A DF-21D and a DF-26B are sitting on an airlift field 600 miles inside the red lodgement, and while they are there no allied carrier operates east of the Banda Sea. They move at first light.
[*][b]Coral Sea Convoy[/b] - Escort an ANL convoy across the Coral Sea. HMAS Hobart, two Anzacs and HMAS Supply shepherd six merchantmen toward Port Moresby while a PLAN diesel patrol line waits somewhere along the track.
[/olist]
